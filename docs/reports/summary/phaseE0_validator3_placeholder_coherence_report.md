**What the two options mean concretely, restated per the task brief**:
- **Option 1 (additive)**: the Type's real-item total (column "Real-item total") is left exactly
  as production Top-down computes it today; each placeholder's Type-mean/median qty is simply
  added on top. The **"Option 1 total" column is what the Type would actually plan for** under
  this treatment — it now **exceeds** what the Type-level Combination model itself forecasts
  (`type_level_direct_forecast_qty_2026_08` in the CSV, equal to the "real-item total" column to
  rounding) by the inflation % shown. **Every real item's own forecast is unchanged.**
- **Option 2 (carve-out)**: the Type's total stays fixed at today's real-item total (the "Option
  2 total" column, identical to "real-item total" by construction — this is the arithmetic
  identity the task asked to confirm, and it holds exactly in every row). Placeholders and real
  items now compete for a share of that same fixed pool, so **every real item's own forecast
  shrinks** by the scale factor `real_item_total / (real_item_total + placeholder_sum)` — see
  `carveout_scale_factor_real_items` in the CSV (ranges from **0.9918** for LED Street light, a
  0.8% shrink, down to **0.1629** for Current Transformer Type CDB, an **83.7% shrink**).

**Two extreme cases worth flagging explicitly (verified from data)**:
- **`Suspension Insulator` (PEM101)**: 6 placeholder items outnumber the 3 real items and their
  combined placeholder qty (883.74/month) is **7x** the real Type total (124.95/month). Under
  Option 1 the Type would plan for 1,008.69 units/month, over 8x what its own Type-level model
  forecasts. Under Option 2, real items shrink to **12.4%** of their current forecast
  (scale factor 0.1239).
- **`Current Transformer Type CDB` (PEM107)**: only 2 of 12 pricelist codes in this Type have any
  history at all (real total: 0.19 units/month — a very thin base), and the other 10 are all
  placeholder items whose combined assigned qty (0.97/month) is over 5x the real total. Option 1
  inflates the Type to 6.1x its modelled total; Option 2 shrinks each real item to **16.3%** of
  its current forecast.
- Both extremes share the same structural cause: a Type with very few, very thin real siblings
  attracting a disproportionately large placeholder population — the placeholder mechanism was
  never designed with an upper bound relative to the Type's own real-item scale, and neither
  option resolves that on its own (Option 1 masks it by inflating the total silently; Option 2
  absorbs it by shrinking real items sharply).

## 5. Which treatment "preserves a coherent hierarchy" — both stated plainly, no side picked

**Option 2 (carve-out) preserves hierarchy consistency exactly** (Type total = sum of item-level
allocations, by construction — confirmed as an arithmetic identity in every one of the 16 rows in
§1-3's table: "Option 2 total" always equals "real-item total" exactly). **Practical implication**:
every real item's own forecast is silently reduced from what Top-down would otherwise give it
today — by as little as 0.8% (LED Street light) or as much as 83.7% (Current Transformer CDB) —
even though nothing about that real item's own historical demand changed. A planner reading only
the real item's forecast would see a number smaller than "what my own history says," with the gap
explained only by other items (the placeholders) they may not even be aware exist in the same
Type.

**Option 1 (additive) leaves every real item's own forecast exactly as it is today** — nothing a
planner already trusts about a real item's number changes. **Practical implication**: the Type's
total (the sum a planner would get by adding up every item in the Type, real and placeholder) now
**exceeds** what the Type-level Combination model itself forecasts for that Type — by 0.8% to
**707.3%** depending on the Type (§1-3). Any downstream use of the Type-level total (capacity
planning, budget roll-ups, a dashboard that sums item forecasts back up to Type) would silently
disagree with the Type-level model's own number, by a margin that varies enormously Type to Type
and is largest exactly where the placeholder population is large relative to a thin real-item
base (Suspension Insulator, CDB, CEL, 22kV Recloser).

**Neither option is chosen here.** This is the same two-sided situation `AGENTS.md` rule 9
describes ("when [there are two positions], the Synthesizer reports both... it does not pick a
side") — both computed outcomes and their implications are reported above; the choice between
"keep every real item's number stable, accept a hierarchy gap at Type level" and "keep the Type
total exact, accept smaller real-item numbers" is left to a human.
